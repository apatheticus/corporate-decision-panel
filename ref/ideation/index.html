The Team of Teams
Agent Skill

A Company OS — Boardroom in a Box. The analytical power of a full executive committee, with the structural integrity of mandated dissent.

Not a chatbot that roleplays executives, but a complete organizational reasoning engine that gives any founder, leader, or team multi-perspective analysis — even when the human user might prefer to hear only good news.

Analytical Frameworks

Pillar 1

The Cascading Deliberation Engine

A five-phase process that decomposes any business issue through a two-tier agent hierarchy with engineered dissent, producing a structured Decision Record that captures the full reasoning chain.

Five-Phase Cascade

When a user presents an issue, it flows through a structured deliberation pipeline. Each layer transforms the question rather than just distributing work — the five phases create a knowledge-generation machine where the analysis itself is more valuable than the final recommendation.

0
Shared Consciousness Broadcast
Before analysis begins, the CEO broadcasts issue context to all activated C-suite agents simultaneously. Everyone sees the same picture before reasoning independently.
1
CEO Frames and Routes
The CEO decomposes the issue into evaluation dimensions, classifies the decision type, and routes to relevant C-suite members. Routing is itself an analytical act.
2
C-Suite Dispatches Downward
Each C-suite executive translates the CEO's framing into domain-specific sub-questions for their team leads. The CFO does not forward the question — they reframe it through their domain lens.
3
Team Leads Produce Findings
Each team lead performs narrow, focused analysis through their specialist lens using their unique analytical framework and mandatory output template.
4
C-Suite Synthesizes Upward
Each C-suite executive collects team lead findings and synthesizes a domain recommendation with confidence level, key risks, and opportunities. Internal contradictions are flagged as analytical signals.
4.5
Pre-Mortem Challenge (Tier 3)
Each C-suite agent reviews all peer recommendations and answers: "Assume this decision fails in 12 months. What caused the failure?" One round only. Research-backed as the highest single-technique value-add for decision quality.
5
CEO Deliberation
The CEO maps all domain recommendations onto a single matrix, identifies fault lines, determines the most determinative perspective, applies the active Decision Mode, and produces the final Decision Record.
Key Insight

Disagreement is engineered, not accidental. The CISO is the Constitutional Skeptic. The VP of Sales is the Revenue Optimist. These structural tensions are features that produce richer analysis than consensus ever could.

Decision Record Output Format

Every deliberation produces a structured, self-contained document with these sections:

  1. Executive Summary — The decision, key reasoning, primary dissent (3-5 sentences)
  2. Issue Statement — The question as originally posed
  3. CEO Framing — Decomposition, activated teams, excluded teams with rationale
  4. Domain Analyses — Per-domain recommendation, confidence level, team lead findings, risks, opportunities
  5. Fault Line Analysis — Points of agreement, contention, pre-mortem findings, unresolved tensions
  6. CEO Decision — Decision, most determinative perspective, weight rationale, conditions, accepted risks
  7. Dissenting Views — Strongest objections preserved for record
  8. Next Steps — Specific actions, implied owners, timelines
  9. Metadata — Complexity, primary domain, dissent level, key assumptions

Pillar 2

The Engagement Model

A two-dimensional control surface — depth (three tiers) and width (five decision modes) — that lets users match engagement to decision weight.

Three Interaction Tiers

Most organizational questions do not need a full board meeting. If the skill only operates at maximum depth, users will invoke it rarely and it becomes a quarterly novelty. The three tiers ensure daily adoption.

TierNameInvocationOutput
1 Hallway Question /consult [role]: [question] Advisory Note (3-5 sentences)
2 Working Session /panel [roles]: [issue] Panel Assessment (~1 page)
3 Board Meeting /deliberate: [issue] Full Decision Record (3-5 pages)
Design Decision

SMB-first default: bias toward Tier 1. Most SMB decisions are fast, informal, and made by one or two people. The skill matches that tempo. Tier 1 is the daily habit; Tier 3 is the deliberate escalation.

Auto-Triage: /evaluate: [issue] presents the issue to the CEO for triage. The CEO assesses scope, impact, and reversibility, then recommends a tier. The user can accept, escalate, or de-escalate.

Pillar 3

Cognitive Forcing and Prompt Architecture

The three-layer prompt architecture that prevents voice collapse — ensuring 29 perspectives produce genuinely different analysis, not the same reasoning with different vocabulary.

The Voice Collapse Problem

Telling one LLM to "think as 5 different people" typically produces 5 paragraphs of the same underlying reasoning with different vocabulary. Simple role assignments ("You are a CFO") show zero measurable improvement in output quality.

The solution is not better persona descriptions — it is forcing each perspective to use different analytical methods that produce structurally different outputs. A GAAP compliance checklist and a three-scenario financial model are fundamentally different analytical artifacts that cannot collapse into the same voice.

Three-Layer Architecture

  • Layer 1 — Team Lead Definitions: 29 analytical framework packages, each with a unique methodology, mandatory output template, three forcing questions, accountability framing, and blind spot declaration.
  • Layer 2 — C-Suite Orchestration: 7 domain orchestration templates supporting three cognitive modes: direct consult (Tier 1), full analysis (Tier 2/3), and challenge phase (Phase 4.5).
  • Layer 3 — CEO Synthesis: Fixed analytical component (fault line mapping, cross-domain pre-mortem) plus swappable Decision Mode module.

Three Forcing Question Types

  • Pre-Mortem (Klein 2007): "Assume this decision fails in 12 months. From your domain perspective, what caused the failure?"
  • Adversarial Empathy (McChrystal JSOC): "If you were [domain-relevant external adversary], how would you exploit this?"
  • Domain Devil's Advocate (Nemeth 2001): "What would [domain expert critic] find concerning?"

Pillar 4

Decision Modes and the Decision Space Map

Five configurable CEO synthesis modes transform the skill from a decision engine into a decision exploration tool — revealing the decision space, not just a single recommendation.

Core Principle

The same domain analysis can be synthesized through multiple Decision Modes. By running the same inputs through different synthesis styles, the user sees not "what to do" but "what the terrain looks like in every direction."

Guardian
MaxiMin — Risk-Averse
Weights skeptic roles heavily. Skeptics must be satisfied, not just acknowledged. Decisions tend toward: don't do it, do a smaller version, or do it with extensive guardrails.
Pioneer
MaxiMax — Growth-Oriented
Weights advocate roles heavily. Skeptic concerns are treated as engineering problems to solve, not reasons to stop. Decisions tend toward: do it, do it bigger, do it faster.
Architect
Behavioral — Consensus-Building
Weights the fault lines themselves. Seeks the position that satisfies the most domain concerns. Conditions drawn from multiple domains.
Analyst
Hurwicz Balanced — Default
Weights confidence levels regardless of role disposition. High-confidence findings carry more weight. "Defer pending better data" is a legitimate outcome.
Sentinel
MiniMax Regret — Regret-Minimizing
Disproportionately weights the strongest objection from any role. Asks: "If this goes wrong, which warning will I wish I'd heeded?" Favors survivable paths.
Multi-Mode Comparison — The Power Feature

The key efficiency insight: domain analysis is mode-independent. Multi-mode comparison runs domain analysis once (the expensive part) and CEO synthesis multiple times (cheap, single-agent passes). Cost: approximately 1.1x a single deliberation for 5x the strategic insight.

Invocation examples:

  • /deliberate guardian: should we acquire CompetitorX?
  • /deliberate guardian vs pioneer: should we acquire CompetitorX?
  • /deliberate all-modes: should we acquire CompetitorX?
  • /consult cfo guardian: can we afford this?

Mode Sensitivity is a novel signal: if all modes produce the same decision, the right answer does not depend on the user's posture. If modes diverge dramatically, the user knows their personal risk appetite is the deciding factor, not the analysis. This transforms a binary recommendation into a nuanced view of the decision landscape.

Implementation

Technical Specification

A hybrid agent architecture built on Claude Code Agent Teams and custom subagents, with heterogeneous model tiering for cost-optimized multi-perspective analysis.

Hybrid Agent Architecture

  • Tier 1 (Agent Team members): CEO + 7 C-suite officers. Real teammates spawned by the skill orchestrator. Can message each other and the CEO directly.
  • Tier 2 (Custom subagents): 29 team lead specialists defined in .claude/agents/team-leads/. Each C-suite agent invokes their team leads as subagents with their own context window.

This architecture provides real isolation per perspective, cost optimization, and true management simulation where C-suite agents dispatch, collect, and synthesize like real executives.

Company Profile Configuration

The skill supports company profile parameterization that modifies:

  • Roster composition: Conditional roles (e.g., Facilities/Office Manager relevant for manufacturing, not B2B SaaS)
  • Decision mode default: Some companies may default to Guardian (regulated) or Pioneer (startups)
  • Tier escalation thresholds: Conservative companies may auto-escalate more aggressively
  • Industry-specific frameworks: HIPAA for healthcare, SOX for financial services

Default profile: mid-market IT/technology services company, 200-500 employees.

Onboarding Stress Test: When first configured, the skill runs a representative cross-functional issue through the full cascade. This validates the configuration, calibrates the user, and surfaces profile issues.

Looking Ahead

Future Directions

Directions where the skill evolves from a decision-making tool into a strategic exploration platform and organizational health monitor.

Decision Revisitation

Revisit decisions when assumptions change. Identifies which assumptions have been invalidated, re-runs only affected domain analyses, and produces a Decision Amendment rather than a full new record. Transforms the skill from a point-in-time tool into a decision lifecycle manager.

Scenario Forking Engine

Parallel cascades with different parameters producing comparative decision landscapes. Not just "what should we do?" but "what happens if we do X vs. Y vs. Z?" Output: a Decision Landscape showing how different assumptions lead to different outcomes.

Organizational Stress Test

Invert the cascade for bottom-up threat and opportunity discovery. Each C-suite agent generates issues from their domain: "What keeps you up at night? What opportunity are we missing?" Output: an Organizational Health Report. Getting a full physical instead of going to the doctor with a complaint.

Institutional Memory

Persistent knowledge across sessions. Roles maintain context about previous decisions, the company's evolving constraints. Previous Decision Records inform current analysis. The system learns the company's patterns and can reference them.

Cross-Functional Deliberation

Beyond the one-round pre-mortem, a full cross-functional challenge where C-suite agents debate each other's recommendations. Requires careful scoping around specific fault lines to prevent "problem drift."

Framework Versioning

Analytical framework packages versioned and improved independently. Industry-specific variants override defaults. Community-contributed domain frameworks. The skill becomes a platform for organizational reasoning patterns.

Open Questions

Unresolved Tensions Worth Carrying Forward

Questions surfaced during the ideation session that deserve further exploration before or during implementation.

  • Tier escalation mid-conversation: Can a Tier 1 consult escalate to Tier 2 if the C-suite agent realizes the question is bigger than it seemed? How does the system signal and handle this gracefully?
  • Custom blended modes: Could users create composite synthesis styles (e.g., "growth-oriented but weight security concerns heavily")? How do you prevent mode proliferation from diluting the framework's clarity?
  • Tier 1 internalization quality: In direct consult mode, the C-suite agent reasons through team lead lenses internally without subagent isolation. How well does this work in practice versus full delegation?
  • Framework specificity vs. generality: The CFO domain has 5 complete packages. Can the remaining 24 be written with equal analytical specificity, or do some domains resist being framed as structured methodologies?
  • Cross-domain forcing questions: Should team leads have forcing questions that explicitly reference other domains' assumptions? This adds richness but increases coupling.
  • Institutional memory scope: How much session-to-session persistence is valuable before the skill becomes brittle from accumulated context? Where is the boundary between helpful memory and stale assumption?
