# Research: Complete Org Roster & Decision Record Format

**Requested by:** Grounder (explicit request for full team lead roster and decision record format)
**Date:** 2026-02-22

## Question
1. What are the complete team lead rosters for all 7 C-suite/VP roles in a mid-market IT/technology services company?
2. What should the decision record format look like as the primary deliverable of the skill?

---

## Part 1: Complete Organizational Roster

### Design Assumptions
- Company type: Mid-market IT/technology services company (200-500 employees)
- CEO has 7 direct reports: COO, CFO, CAO, CTO, CISO, VP of Sales, VP of Delivery
- Each direct report has 4-6 team leads performing key functions
- Total roles: 1 CEO + 7 C-suite + ~35 team leads = ~43 agents

### The Full Roster

---

#### CEO (Chief Executive Officer)
**Mandate:** Strategic vision, final decision authority, stakeholder management
**Disposition:** Integrative synthesizer -- weighs all perspectives, identifies fault lines, resolves conflicts
**Direct Reports:** COO, CFO, CAO, CTO, CISO, VP of Sales, VP of Delivery

---

#### COO (Chief Operating Officer)
**Mandate:** Day-to-day operations, process optimization, cross-department execution
**Disposition:** EXECUTION REALIST -- "Can we actually do this with the people and processes we have?"
**Dissent type:** Mandated skeptic (operational feasibility)

| # | Team Lead Role | Domain | Key Analytical Questions |
|---|---------------|--------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Operations Manager | Process efficiency, workflow, resource utilization | "How does this affect our current operational workflows? What processes need to change?" |
| 2 | Project/Program Management Lead | Project delivery, timelines, resource allocation | "What does the implementation timeline look like? What resources get pulled from current projects?" |
| 3 | Process Improvement / Lean Lead | Continuous improvement, waste reduction, efficiency metrics | "Where are the inefficiencies? How do we measure success? What gets simpler or more complex?" |
| 4 | Quality Assurance Manager | Quality standards, SLA compliance, service quality metrics | "Does this affect our quality standards? What are the quality risks during transition?" |
| 5 | Vendor/Procurement Manager | Third-party relationships, supply chain, vendor management | "What vendor dependencies does this create? What are the procurement implications?" |

---

#### CFO (Chief Financial Officer)
**Mandate:** Financial strategy, budgeting, risk assessment, capital allocation
**Disposition:** COST ARCHAEOLOGIST -- "What are the costs that aren't in the proposal?"
**Dissent type:** Mandated skeptic (financial risk, hidden costs)

| # | Team Lead Role | Domain | Key Analytical Questions |
|---|---------------|--------|--------------------------|

| 5 | Tax Lead | Tax strategy, compliance, structure optimization (+ external firm) | "What are the tax implications? Is there a more tax-efficient structure? Any compliance triggers?" |

### Dissent Balance
- **Mandated Skeptics (4):** COO, CFO, CISO, VP of Delivery -- these roles structurally challenge proposals
- **Advocacy Roles (2):** CTO, VP of Sales -- these roles structurally champion growth and capability
- **Neutral/Balanced (1):** CAO -- leans skeptic on organizational capacity but neutral on strategy
- **Synthesizer (1):** CEO -- integrates all perspectives

This 4:2:1 skeptic-to-advocate ratio ensures proposals are thoroughly stress-tested. Even genuinely good ideas must survive financial, operational, security, and delivery scrutiny before approval. This reflects real organizational dynamics where it's harder to get approval than to propose ideas.

## Part 2: Decision Record Format

### Design Rationale
The decision record is the primary deliverable the skill produces. It must:
1. Capture the full multi-perspective analysis (not just the final decision)
2. Show the reasoning chain (how the decision was reached, not just what was decided)
3. Preserve dissenting views (the CISO's objections don't disappear just because the CEO decided to proceed)
4. Be actionable (include next steps, conditions, and risk mitigations)
5. Be referenceable (support future decisions by showing precedent)

The format draws from three established templates:
- **Architecture Decision Records (ADR)** -- from software engineering, emphasis on context, decision, consequences
- **DACI Framework** -- from Atlassian, emphasis on roles (Driver, Approver, Contributors, Informed)
- **McChrystal Red Team Output** -- emphasis on adversarial analysis and competing hypotheses

### Key Design Choices in This Format

1. **Section 3 preserves individual executive analysis** -- the user can read any single executive's assessment independently. This is the "shared consciousness" made visible.

2. **Section 4 (Fault Line Analysis) is unique** -- it doesn't just list recommendations; it identifies WHERE and WHY executives disagreed. This is the highest-value section for the user because it reveals the trade-offs that aren't obvious from any single perspective.

3. **Section 5 (Red Team / Pre-Mortem) is mandatory** -- every decision gets at least a basic pre-mortem. This is the cognitive forcing function that prevents groupthink even when most executives agree.

4. **Section 6 preserves dissenting views** -- even in an APPROVED decision, the CISO's security concerns or the CFO's cost objections are recorded. This serves two purposes: (a) it signals to the user that these concerns exist and may need monitoring, (b) it creates an audit trail for future reference.

5. **Section 6 shows weighting** -- the user can see HOW the CEO weighted different perspectives, making the synthesis transparent rather than opaque.

6. **Metadata tracks analysis depth** -- so the user knows whether this was a quick assessment or a thorough multi-layer analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- **The 43-role roster is now concrete** with specific team leads, domains, and analytical questions for each. This gives the builder agent exactly what it needs to write system prompts.
- **The dissent balance (4 skeptics : 2 advocates : 1 neutral : 1 synthesizer) ensures structural stress-testing.** Every proposal faces financial, operational, security, and delivery scrutiny.
- **Each team lead has defined "Key Analytical Questions"** -- these translate directly into cognitive forcing functions in the system prompts. The builder agent can use these as checklist items.
- **The decision record has 8 sections** with the Fault Line Analysis (Section 4) and Pre-Mortem (Section 5) being the highest-value unique sections that differentiate this from standard decision documentation.
- **Dissenting views are preserved, not suppressed** -- this is a core design principle that reflects McChrystal's shared consciousness AND good organizational governance.
