# Research: SMB Organizational Decision-Making Patterns

**Requested by:** Arbiter (initial research assignment)
**Date:** 2026-02-22

## Question
How do SMBs typically structure decision-making? What are common pain points? How do C-suite roles interact in practice at small/medium companies?

## Findings

### SMB Definition and Scale
- **Small business**: Typically 1-100 employees, revenue under $50M
- **Medium business (mid-market)**: 100-999 employees, revenue $50M-$1B
- The concept seed describes a structure with CEO + 7 direct reports + ~35 team leads, suggesting a mid-market company of approximately 200-500 employees

### Typical SMB Organizational Structures

**Flat Structure** (very small, <25 employees): CEO/founder at top with 1-2 layers below. All decisions flow through the owner. Most common in startups.

**Hierarchical Structure** (25-500 employees): CEO at top, departments below with directors/managers/staff. The concept seed's structure fits this model -- it's the standard mid-market org chart with two management tiers below the CEO.

**Trend**: 85% of leaders in Deloitte's 2025 Global Human Capital Trends report said their companies need greater agility. More SMBs are adopting flatter, collaborative structures.

### C-Suite Role Profiles (Mapped to Concept Seed)

#### CEO (Chief Executive Officer)
- **Domain**: Long-term strategy, vision, external stakeholder management
- **Decision style**: Final authority. Evaluates recommendations from direct reports.
- **SMB reality**: In smaller firms, deeply hands-on. In mid-market, more strategic.
- **Key interactions**: Directly manages all C-suite; sets agenda for cross-functional issues

#### COO (Chief Operating Officer)
- **Domain**: Day-to-day operations, process optimization, cross-department coordination
- **Decision style**: Execution-focused. Translates CEO strategy into operational reality.
- **SMB reality**: Often second-in-command; may overlap with CEO in smaller firms
- **Key interactions**: Works closely with all departments; ensures alignment between strategy and execution
- **Typical team leads**: Operations Manager, Project Management Lead, Process Improvement Lead, Quality Assurance Manager

#### CFO (Chief Financial Officer)
- **Domain**: Financial strategy, budgeting, revenue/profitability, financial controls, risk assessment
- **Decision style**: Data-driven, risk-aware. Evaluates financial implications of all decisions.
- **SMB reality**: May be outsourced or fractional in very small firms; critical in mid-market
- **Key interactions**: Close collaboration with CEO on strategy, COO on operational budgets, CTO on tech investments
- **Typical team leads** (from concept seed): Controller (accounting/close), Head of FP&A (planning/analytics), Treasury/Cash Manager, AP Manager, AR Manager, Tax Accountant (internal lead + external firm)

#### CTO (Chief Technology Officer)
- **Domain**: Technology strategy, product development, system architecture, technical teams, innovation
- **Decision style**: Technology-forward but must balance innovation with practicality and security
- **SMB reality**: Critical for tech companies; may be part-time or outsourced for non-tech SMBs
- **Key interactions**: Collaborates with CISO on security, COO on tech operations, CFO on tech budgets
- **Typical team leads**: Engineering Lead, DevOps/Infrastructure Lead, Product Development Lead, QA/Testing Lead, Solutions Architect

#### CISO (Chief Information Security Officer)
- **Domain**: Information security strategy, risk assessment, incident response, compliance, data protection
- **Decision style**: Risk-focused, compliance-oriented. Often seen as the "no" voice that needs to be balanced.
- **SMB reality**: Many smaller SMBs assign CISO duties to CTO or a Director of Security. Mid-market increasingly has a dedicated CISO.
- **Key interactions**: Works closely with CTO on security architecture, CFO on security investment, CAO on compliance
- **Typical team leads**: Security Operations Lead, Compliance/GRC Lead, Identity & Access Management Lead, Incident Response Lead

#### CAO (Chief Administrative Officer)
- **Domain**: Administrative backbone -- HR, legal, compliance, IT operations, facilities, organizational policies
- **Decision style**: Process-oriented, compliance-focused, people-centric
- **SMB reality**: In smaller firms, this is a "jack-of-all-trades" generalist. In mid-market, increasingly formalized.
- **Key interactions**: Works with all departments on HR needs, ensures legal/regulatory compliance across the org
- **Typical team leads**: HR Director, Legal Counsel/Compliance Lead, Facilities Manager, Office/Admin Manager, Procurement Lead

#### VP of Sales
- **Domain**: Revenue generation, sales strategy, customer acquisition, pipeline management, team quotas
- **Decision style**: Revenue-driven, customer-focused, competitive. Often champions growth over caution.
- **SMB reality**: Critical role; directly impacts revenue. Reports to CEO or COO depending on structure.
- **Key interactions**: Close collaboration with VP of Delivery (service promises), CFO (revenue forecasting), CTO (product capabilities)
- **Typical team leads**: Sales Managers (by region/vertical), Business Development Lead, Sales Operations/Enablement Lead, Account Management Lead

#### VP of Delivery (Service Delivery)
- **Domain**: Service delivery, client satisfaction, project execution, resource allocation, SLA management
- **Decision style**: Client-satisfaction focused, operationally rigorous, quality-oriented
- **SMB reality**: Critical for services companies; may not exist in pure product companies
- **Key interactions**: Tight coupling with VP of Sales (what was sold vs. what can be delivered), COO (resource allocation), CTO (technical delivery)
- **Typical team leads**: Delivery Managers, Client Success Lead, Resource/Capacity Planning Lead, Technical Project Leads

### Decision-Making Patterns in SMBs

1. **Concentrated Authority**: SMBs typically have only 1-3 actual decision-makers, with the business owner almost always being one. Even with a full C-suite, the CEO often has final say on most significant decisions.

2. **Speed Over Process**: Decision-making is often quick and influenced by immediate emotional, aspirational, and market-driven challenges. Less formal governance compared to enterprise.

3. **Cross-Functional Tension**: The fundamental tensions in SMB decision-making:
   - **Growth vs. Risk**: VP Sales pushes growth; CFO/CISO flag risk
   - **Speed vs. Quality**: COO wants efficiency; VP Delivery wants thoroughness
   - **Innovation vs. Stability**: CTO pushes new tech; CISO/CFO push caution
   - **People vs. Budget**: CAO advocates for people investment; CFO guards the budget

4. **Personal Relationships Matter**: In SMBs, trust and personal relationships drive decision-making more than formal process. This aligns strongly with McChrystal's Trust pillar.

5. **Hands-On C-Suite**: Unlike enterprise, SMB C-suite members are often deeply involved in day-to-day operations, not just strategy. They have intimate knowledge of frontline issues.

### Common Pain Points

1. **Information Silos**: Departments don't share information effectively. Each leader sees only their domain. This is exactly what shared consciousness is designed to solve.

2. **Decision Bottlenecks**: Everything flows to the CEO for approval, creating delays. McChrystal's empowered execution addresses this.

3. **Resource Constraints**: Limited budgets mean trade-offs between departments. CFO often becomes the bottleneck.

4. **Lack of Formal Analysis**: Decisions are made on gut feeling rather than structured analysis from multiple perspectives. The Team of Teams agent could address this directly.

5. **Alignment Drift**: Without regular cross-functional communication, teams drift apart in priorities and understanding.

6. **Reactive vs. Proactive**: SMBs often respond to crises rather than anticipating them. Structured evaluation processes could help.

### How the Concept Seed's Org Structure Maps to Real SMBs

The proposed structure (CEO -> 7 C-suite/VP -> team leads) represents a **well-structured mid-market services company**, likely in IT/technology services given the inclusion of both CISO and VP of Delivery. This structure is realistic for a company of 200-500 employees. Key observations:

- The inclusion of both CTO and CISO suggests a technology-forward company that takes security seriously
- VP of Sales + VP of Delivery is a classic services company pairing
- CAO rather than CHRO suggests a broader administrative scope
- The team lead layer (Controller, FP&A, etc.) represents realistic mid-management roles

## Key Takeaways
- **The concept seed's org structure is realistic and well-suited to a mid-market IT/technology services company** of 200-500 employees. The role definitions map to real-world responsibilities.
- **The fundamental cross-functional tensions (growth vs. risk, speed vs. quality, innovation vs. stability) are the engine** that makes multi-perspective evaluation valuable. Each C-suite role will naturally champion different priorities.
- **SMB decision-making is faster and more personal than enterprise**, meaning the agent simulation should reflect quick, opinionated responses informed by deep domain knowledge, not lengthy corporate analysis.
- **Information silos are the #1 pain point** in SMB decision-making -- and shared consciousness is the direct solution. The agent skill's value proposition is essentially "McChrystal's shared consciousness, delivered by AI."
- **Each role's team leads should bring granular, specialized expertise** (e.g., the CFO's Tax Accountant spotting tax implications the CFO might miss). This multi-level analysis is what makes the concept unique.
