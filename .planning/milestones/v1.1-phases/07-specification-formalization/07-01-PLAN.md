1. A subsection header (### N. ThresholdName)
2. A **Core question** (one sentence framing the threshold as a diagnostic question)
3. A diagnostic questions table with 2-3 rows, each containing:
   - A concrete, binary diagnostic question about the decision
   - A YES column explaining why YES means the threshold is triggered
   - A NO column explaining why NO means the threshold is not triggered
4. A trigger rule: "If ANY diagnostic question answers YES: Threshold triggered -- full activation."
5. 2-3 **Calibration Exemplars** showing concrete business decisions:
   - At least 1 clear YES (triggers the threshold) with reasoning
   - At least 1 clear NO (does not trigger) with reasoning
   - Optionally 1 borderline case illustrating the judgment involved

The 5 thresholds and their diagnostic dimensions (from research):

**1. Irreversibility**
Core question: Can this decision be meaningfully reversed within 12 months at acceptable cost?
Diagnostics: capability destruction, regulatory re-approval, cost-of-reversal exceeding 50% of original.
Exemplars: YES = "Divest our healthcare division"; NO = "Switch project management tools from Jira to Linear"; Borderline = "Commit to 3-year cloud vendor contract with early termination fee"

**2. Headcount Impact**
Core question: Does this decision affect more than 30% of current headcount through direct or cascading effects?
Diagnostics: direct headcount change, cascading effects on adjacent teams, role elimination vs redeployment.
Exemplars: YES = "Reduce engineering from 100 to 40 through outsourcing"; NO = "Hire 5 new sales reps"; Borderline = "Merge two 50-person departments into one 70-person department"

**3. Market Position Change**
Core question: Does this decision alter how the company is positioned in its market or how it generates revenue?
Diagnostics: customer segment change, revenue model change, competitive positioning shift.
Exemplars: YES = "Pivot from B2B SaaS to B2C marketplace"; NO = "Launch a premium tier of existing product"; Borderline = "Acquire a competitor to consolidate market share"

**4. Existential Financial Risk**
Core question: Could this decision, if it fails, threaten the company's ability to continue operating?
Diagnostics: committed capital exceeding runway threshold, single-source dependency, debt-to-equity impact.
Exemplars: YES = "Invest 80% of cash reserves in new product line"; NO = "Increase marketing spend by 20%"; Borderline = "Take on debt equal to annual revenue for expansion"

**5. Domain Uncertainty**
Core question: Is the CEO unable to confidently identify which 2-3 domains are most relevant to this decision?
Diagnostics: precedent availability, domain overlap ambiguity, novel decision category.
Exemplars: YES = "Respond to an AI regulation that affects engineering, legal, sales, and product simultaneously"; NO = "Negotiate a new office lease"; Borderline = "Launch operations in a new country"

Keep the existing "After classifying..." introduction paragraph and the closing "The CEO states activation reasoning..." paragraph. Preserve the Default Activation table (lines 1-14) and CSO Research Activation table (lines 28-41) UNCHANGED.
